Foundations

Dot product, cosine similarity, vector magnitude, and when to use each in AI systems.

01
Dot Product — How it determines similarity
The mathematical operation that powers attention, recommendation, and search.
Apple & Fruit example

The dot product of two vectors is computed by multiplying corresponding components and summing the results. It gives a higher scalar value when vectors point in similar directions and have larger magnitudes.

Formula A · B = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + … + aₙbₙ

Geometric meaning A · B = |A| × |B| × cos(θ)
Key insight: The dot product captures both direction alignment (via cos θ) and magnitude (via |A|·|B|). Cosine similarity strips the magnitude and keeps only direction.
◆ Full calculation walkthrough — Apple vs Fruit ▼
Vectors (2D toy example) Apple → [0.9, 0.1] (food-related, low living-thing) Fruit → [0.8, 0.2] (food-related, low living-thing) Animal → [0.1, 0.9] (low food, high living-thing)
Apple · Fruit = (0.9 × 0.8) + (0.1 × 0.2) = 0.72 + 0.02 = 0.74
Apple · Animal = (0.9 × 0.1) + (0.1 × 0.9) = 0.09 + 0.27 = 0.36
Result: Apple is more similar to Fruit (0.74) than Animal (0.36). The dot product correctly captures this — but only when magnitudes are comparable.

Interpretation of dot product values:

ValueMeaningAngle (θ)
High positiveVectors point in same direction — high similaritySmall (near 0°)
ZeroVectors are perpendicular — unrelated90°
NegativeVectors point in opposite directions — dissimilarNear 180°
02
Cosine Similarity — Why we use cos(θ) not θ
The normalised dot product that removes magnitude bias.
Apple & Fruit example
Cosine Similarity Formula cos_sim(A, B) = A · B / (|A| × |B|)

Range Always between -1 and +1 (or 0 to 1 for word vectors)

We use cos(θ), not the angle θ itself, for three reasons:

  • 1Standard range: cos(θ) stays between -1 and +1. Higher always means more similar — intuitive for data science.
  • 2Inverse relationship fixed: With θ, a smaller angle (0°) means high similarity, which is counter-intuitive. cos(θ) flips this: 1 = identical direction, 0 = unrelated.
  • 3Computational efficiency: Computing the cosine via dot product and magnitudes is faster than computing the actual angle using arccos.
◆ Full calculation — Apple vs Fruit cosine similarity ▼
Step 1: Magnitudes |Apple| = √(0.9² + 0.1²) = √(0.81 + 0.01) = √0.82 ≈ 0.906 |Fruit| = √(0.8² + 0.2²) = √(0.64 + 0.04) = √0.68 ≈ 0.825
Step 2: Cosine similarity = 0.74 / (0.906 × 0.825) = 0.74 / 0.748 ≈ 0.991
0.991 — Apple and Fruit are nearly identical in direction, even though their magnitudes differ slightly. This is what cosine similarity captures that raw dot product misses.
03
The Magnitude Problem — Why dot product can mislead
When large magnitude vectors beat semantically similar ones.
Animal [0.9, 0.9] problem

If Animal had vectors [0.9, 0.9] instead of [0.1, 0.9], the dot product would give it a higher score than Fruit, even though it is less semantically similar to Apple:

Dot Product (raw) Apple · Fruit [0.8,0.2] = 0.74 ✓ Apple · Animal [0.9,0.9] = 1.08 ✗ (Animal wins due to large magnitude)
Cosine Similarity (normalised) Apple vs Fruit ≈ 0.990 ✓ Apple vs Animal ≈ 0.894 ✓ (Fruit correctly wins)
Key insight: Cosine similarity normalises out magnitude. Use it whenever you care only about semantic direction, not strength. Use raw dot product when magnitude is meaningful (e.g., Transformer attention, recommendation systems).
04
Dot Product vs Cosine — When to use which in AI
Production use-case mapping for both similarity metrics.
MetricAI Use CaseWhy
Dot Product Transformer self-attention (LLMs) Speed: no square root or division. Magnitude represents "importance" of a token.
Dot Product Recommendation systems (Spotify, Netflix) Users who interact more (higher magnitude) should have stronger impact.
Cosine Similarity Document search & RAG retrieval Prevents long documents from outscoring short ones just because of word count.
Cosine Similarity Image retrieval Cares about content direction (style/subject), not brightness or contrast magnitude.
05
Dot Product Has No Fixed Range — How similarity is determined
Relative ranking, learned thresholds, and Softmax normalisation.

Unlike cosine similarity, the dot product score can be 10, 500, or -2000 depending on vector magnitudes. Three approaches handle this:

  • 1Relative ranking (search): Don't ask "is 50 good?" — ask "which vector scores highest?" The model returns the top-K results by rank.
  • 2Learned thresholds: In specific systems (face recognition, spam filters), engineers find model-specific thresholds through testing. A threshold of 50 for one model would be meaningless for another.
  • 3Softmax normalisation: Used in Transformers. Converts raw scores into probabilities: [74, 12, 2] → [0.99, 0.01, 0.00]. Now scores look like percentages.
Summary: Dot product is used for ranking (find the best match) rather than scoring (decide if a match is good in isolation). It always requires context or normalisation to be interpretable.
06
Long Document Bias — How cosine fixes the length problem
Why dot product favours long documents over relevant short ones.

The document vector is built by summing or averaging word vectors. A document mentioning "Apple" 50 times accumulates 50× the value:

Document vector accumulation Apple vector: [0.9, 0.1] Doc (1 mention): [0.9, 0.1] → dot product = 0.82 Doc (50 mentions): [45.0, 5.0] → dot product = 41.0
The 50× bias 50-mention doc scores ~50× higher than 1-mention doc even if the 1-mention doc is perfectly relevant

Dot product says

"This textbook is huge and mentions Apple 50 times → score: 500"

Biased toward length.

Cosine similarity says

500 / 5,000 (length) = 0.1 (low score).
Short doc: 10 / 5 = 2.0 (high score).

Normalised by length.

Key insight: Dot product measures "how much of word X is in this document?" Cosine similarity measures "what percentage of this document is about word X?" In text search, use cosine.
07
Query vs Document Vectors — Same dimensions, always
How query and document vectors are compared in practice.
Critical rule: Both vectors must have the same number of dimensions. A 2D query vector cannot be compared to a 1000D document vector. The embedding model fixes the dimensionality — everything uses the same size.

Documents are compressed into a single fixed-size vector via averaging or [CLS] token. The dot product is always element-wise: position 1 × position 1, position 2 × position 2. Never cross-multiplied.

◆ Full example — "red fruit" document averaging to 2D ▼
Document: "An apple a day keeps the doctor away" "an" → [0.1, 0.1] "apple" → [0.9, 0.1] "a" → [0.1, 0.1] "day" → [0.2, 0.2] "keeps" → [0.1, 0.1] "the" → [0.1, 0.1] "doctor" → [0.5, 0.2] "away" → [0.1, 0.1]
Average → [0.263, 0.125]
Query: "apple" → [0.9, 0.1] Dot product = (0.9 × 0.263) + (0.1 × 0.125) = 0.237 + 0.013 = 0.25
Cosine similarity ≈ 0.947 (high directional match)

Embeddings

How words become vectors: context, training, and what the 768 dimensions actually mean.

08
How Embeddings Capture Meaning
Why similar words end up with similar vectors.

When BERT trains on billions of sentences, it notices that "apple" and "fruit" appear in similar contexts:

"I ate an apple" and "I ate a fruit" "The apple was sweet" and "The fruit was sweet"

Because they appear in similar contexts, the model pushes their vectors to point in a similar direction. Context defines meaning, and meaning defines direction.

Key insight: In a 2D space with dimensions D1="food-related-ness" and D2="living-thing-related-ness", Apple [0.9, 0.3] and Fruit [0.8, 0.4] both point toward D1. Animal [0.1, 0.9] points toward D2 — a very different direction.
09
BERT Training — Masked Language Modelling (MLM)
How BERT learns by predicting hidden words.

BERT uses Masked Language Modelling: it hides some words and asks the model to predict them. This is a classification problem — not a vector prediction.

MLM Training examples Input: "I ate an [MASK] for breakfast" Target: "apple" (from vocabulary of ~30,000 words)
Input: "The [MASK] was sweet and red" Target: "apple"

Classic ML analogy

  • Predictor variables: fixed features (age, salary)
  • Response variable: target to predict
  • Weights: learned during training

BERT equivalent

  • Predictor variables: other word vectors (being learned)
  • Response variable: the masked word label
  • Weights: embedding table + Transformer weights
Key difference: In classic ML, predictor values are fixed input data. In BERT, the word vectors ARE the parameters being learned — they serve dual purpose as both input and what the model adjusts.
10
The Embedding Table — Random start, meaningful end
What the model actually learns and how it converges.

Every word in BERT's vocabulary (~30,000 words) starts with a random 768D vector. These mean nothing initially. Meaning emerges through training.

Evolution of "apple" vector during training Initialisation: [ 0.23, -0.87, 0.41, … ] (random) After 1M steps: [ 0.91, 0.10, 0.73, … ] (converging) After 1B steps: [ 0.94, 0.12, 0.71, … ] (stable, meaningful)

11
768 Dimensions — What they actually encode
No human assigns meaning. Dimensions are emergent and entangled.

2D toy example (human-assigned)

D1 = food-related-ness (clean)
D2 = living-thing-related-ness (clean)

BERT 768D reality (emergent)

D1 = 0.3×food + 0.2×animate + 0.1×colour + …
D2 = 0.4×syntax_role + 0.15×sentiment + …

Researchers have probed BERT's layers and found that groups of dimensions capture interpretable patterns — but no single dimension maps to a clean concept.

Layers 1–4 Syntax: word order, basic grammar, positional relationships
Layers 5–8 Semantics: phrase structure, subject-verb relationships
Layers 9–12 Deep meaning: coreference, word sense, semantic roles
Why 768? Empirically found to balance capacity (enough to encode syntax, semantics, context) and efficiency (not so large that training becomes intractable). BERT-large uses 1,024D.
12
Contextual Embeddings — Same word, different vectors
BERT's key advantage over older Word2Vec models.
Apple — two very different meanings "I bought an Apple laptop" "Apple" → [ 0.21, 0.88, 0.43, … ] (tech context dominant)
"I ate a fresh Apple" "Apple" → [ 0.91, 0.12, 0.73, … ] (food context dominant)

Word2Vec (older)

One fixed 768D vector per word regardless of context. "Apple" always the same.

BERT (contextual)

Different vector per usage based on surrounding words. "Apple" changes with context.

13
Embedding Pipeline — From corpus to RAG retrieval
How embeddings are used in production AI systems including RAG.

After training, the model becomes a fixed embedding engine. Text in, vector out. Used for search, recommendation, and RAG retrieval.

Offline (document ingestion — done once) PDF/Doc → Text extraction → Chunking (200-500 tokens) → Each chunk → BERT → 768D vector → Stored in Vector DB
Online (query time) User query → BERT → Single 768D vector → Cosine similarity vs all stored vectors → Top-K chunks retrieved → Sent to LLM → Final answer
Key rule: Vector DB stores one vector per chunk, not per document. A 5,000-word document becomes N chunk vectors. Retrieval finds the most relevant chunks, not the most relevant document. Chunk size and overlap are critical architecture decisions.
ScenarioFinal vector output
Single sentence1 × 768D (via [CLS] token or mean pooling)
Large documentN × 768D (one per chunk, stored separately)
User query paragraph1 × 768D (fits in 512 token limit)
Query exceeds limitTruncate or hierarchical average

Transformer Internals

Self-attention, Q/K/V matrices, 12 layers, and what backpropagation actually adjusts.

14
Self-Attention — The core problem it solves
Why every word needs to "look at" every other word simultaneously.

Consider these two sentences:

"The animal didn't cross the street because it was too tired" → "it" = animal
"The animal didn't cross the street because it was too narrow" → "it" = street

Humans resolve this from context. Self-attention lets every token query every other token and decide how much attention to pay to each — simultaneously, in both directions (in BERT).

Key point: This is fundamentally different from older RNN models that read left-to-right sequentially. The Transformer sees the entire sentence at once via self-attention.
15
Q, K, V Mechanism — The library search analogy
How Query, Key, and Value matrices enable contextual attention.
ComponentLibrary analogyIn BERT
Query (Q)Your search query"What am I looking for?"
Key (K)Book index/catalogue"What do I contain?"
Value (V)Actual book content"What do I contribute?"

Every word simultaneously acts as all three. The weight matrices Wq, Wk, Wv (each 768×768 in BERT-base) transform the word vector into Q, K, V representations.

Matrix sizes in BERT-base Word vector (1 × 768) × Wq (768 × 768) = Q (1 × 768) Word vector (1 × 768) × Wk (768 × 768) = K (1 × 768) Word vector (1 × 768) × Wv (768 × 768) = V (1 × 768)
Parameters per layer (attention only): Wq + Wk + Wv = 3 × (768 × 768) ≈ 1.77M parameters Total across 12 layers ≈ 21.2M parameters
◆ Full 2D walkthrough — "red fruit" attention calculation ▼
Step 1: Compute Q, K, V for each word Wq = [[0.5,0.3],[0.2,0.7]] Wk = [[0.4,0.2],[0.3,0.8]] Wv = [[0.6,0.1],[0.2,0.9]]
Q_fruit = [0.26, 0.62] K_fruit = [0.32, 0.68] V_fruit = [0.28, 0.74] Q_red = [0.47, 0.34] K_red = [0.39, 0.26] V_red = [0.56, 0.18]
Step 2: Attention scores (Q · K) Score(fruit→red) = (0.26×0.39)+(0.62×0.26) = 0.262 Score(fruit→fruit) = (0.26×0.32)+(0.62×0.68) = 0.505 After Softmax: fruit attends 44% to "red", 56% to itself
Step 3: New contextual vector for "fruit" = 0.44 × V_red + 0.56 × V_fruit = 0.44×[0.56,0.18] + 0.56×[0.28,0.74] = [0.403, 0.493] (original: [0.2, 0.8] — shifted toward red)
What happened: "Fruit" is no longer just [0.2, 0.8]. After attending to "red", it now carries information about being a red thing. Its vector shifted. This is one layer of self-attention doing its work.
16
What Backpropagation Adjusts in BERT
Two distinct parameter types, adjusted simultaneously.

For each training step on one sentence, two separate things are updated:

Type 1: Embedding Table

Only the rows for words that appeared in the current sentence get nudged. Other words are untouched for this step.

30,000 words × 768D = 23M parameters

Type 2: Transformer Weights (Wq, Wk, Wv)

All 12 layers' weight matrices are updated for every training step, regardless of which words appeared.

12 layers × ~7.1M each ≈ 87M parameters

Key insight: The Transformer weights are general-purpose language reasoning rules — like grammar. The embedding table is the dictionary. Both are learned. Both are frozen after training.
Total BERT-base parameters Embedding table: ~23M Transformer layers: ~87M Total: ~110M parameters
17
Layer Specialisation — Emergent, never configured
Why early layers capture syntax and late layers capture semantics — with no human instruction.

No configuration says "Layer 1, learn grammar." Specialisation emerges naturally because each layer can only build on what the previous layer produced:

Input (raw embeddings) No context yet — pure word identity
Layers 1–4 Simple local patterns → basic syntax, adjacent word relationships
Layers 5–8 Phrase structure, subject-verb relationships, syntactic trees
Layers 9–12 Abstract semantics: word sense, coreference, entity roles
Is it a black box? Partially. The mathematics are fully transparent. The internal representations are studied via probing classifiers (Explainable AI / Mechanistic Interpretability research). Anthropic does significant research in this area. But we still cannot fully explain why specific dimension values emerge.
18
Multi-Head Attention — Multiple perspectives simultaneously
Why BERT runs 8 attention heads in parallel per layer.

Instead of one Q/K/V attention pass, BERT-base runs 8 attention heads simultaneously. Each head learns to attend to different aspects of the sentence:

  • H1Grammatical relationships ("is" → "blue")
  • H2Semantic similarity ("sky" → "blue")
  • H3Positional proximity (neighbouring words)
  • H8Long-range dependencies across sentence

All heads run simultaneously. Their outputs are concatenated and projected back to the original dimension (768D).

Result: Multiple perspectives on the same sentence, processed in parallel. More robust contextualisation than a single attention pass.

Encoder vs Decoder — Two different jobs
Why BERT is encoder-only and GPT/Claude are decoder-only.

BERTEncoder onlyUnderstanding, embedding, retrievalBidirectional (sees full sentence)
GPT / Claude / GeminiDecoder onlyGeneration, next token predictionLeft-to-right masked only

Why decoder-only won for modern LLMs: The prompt tokens serve as the "encoding" — they are all processed in the same self-attention space as the generated tokens. No separate component needed for same-language tasks. This scales better and handles all tasks through careful prompting.
20
Original Transformer (2017) — Encoder + Decoder
Designed for machine translation. "Attention Is All You Need."

The original Transformer needed both components because translation requires two distinct operations:

Translation pipeline Input: "The sky is blue" (English)
ENCODER → Bidirectional attention → Rich contextual vectors for all 4 tokens
DECODER → Masked attention + cross-attention → Generates "Le ciel est bleu"
Output: "Le ciel est bleu" (French)

Decoder has THREE attention operations per layer:

  • 1Masked self-attention: Generated tokens attend to previous generated tokens only (left-to-right).
  • 2Cross-attention: Decoder Q × Encoder K and V. This is the bridge. "bleu" attends most to "blue" in the source. Q from decoder, K and V from encoder output.
  • 3Feed-forward: Standard transformation applied per token position.

22
Positional Encoding — Teaching word order
Why embeddings alone cannot distinguish "dog bites man" from "man bites dog".

After embedding, the model sees N vectors with no positional information. "The sky is blue" and "blue is sky The" produce identical sets of vectors. Positional encoding adds a unique signal per position:

Position signal added to each vector Position 1 ("The"): [0.1, 0.2] + [0.00, 0.10] = [0.10, 0.30] Position 2 ("sky"): [0.5, 0.8] + [0.09, 0.10] = [0.59, 0.90] Position 3 ("is"): [0.2, 0.1] + [0.14, 0.00] = [0.34, 0.10] Position 4 ("blue"): [0.9, 0.3] + [0.09, -0.10] = [0.99, 0.20]
Key detail: Positional encoding values are not learned — they are fixed mathematical values using sine and cosine functions. Each position gets a unique pattern the model learns to recognise.
23
Decoder-Only Models — The prompt IS the encoding
Clarifying a common misconception: embedding table still exists.
Common misconception: "Decoder-only means no text-to-vector conversion." Wrong. Every Transformer model has an embedding table. The decoder-only model still converts tokens to vectors first.

What decoder-only models lack is a separate encoder component with its own independent layers. The difference is the attention pattern, not whether embedding happens:

Decoder-only forward pass (GPT/Claude) User prompt: "The sky is"
Step 1: Embedding table lookup (same as BERT) "The" → [768D], "sky" → [768D], "is" → [768D]
Step 2: Masked self-attention (left-to-right only) "sky" can see "The" ✓ "sky" cannot see "is" ✗
Step 3: Predict next token from last position vector → probability distribution over vocabulary → "blue"
Step 4: Append "blue", repeat

Encoder + Decoder (translation)

Like two people: one reads English aloud, other listens and writes French. Two distinct components, two distinct processes.

Decoder Only (GPT/Claude)

Like one person who reads the prompt and continues writing. Same component, same attention space.

24
Text Generation — One token at a time
How LLMs generate coherent text through next-token prediction and sampling.

Decoder models are trained on one task: given everything so far, predict the next token. Generation then runs this in a loop:

Generation loop — "The sky is" Step 1: Input [The][sky][is] Output: blue=0.42, clear=0.28, dark=0.15 → select "blue"
Step 2: Input [The][sky][is][blue] Output: .=0.28, and=0.31, today=0.22 → select "."
Step 3: Input [The][sky][is][blue][.] Output: [EOS]=0.91 → stop

Sampling strategies:

StrategyBehaviourUse case
Greedy (temp=0)Always picks highest probabilityFactual tasks, deterministic output
Top-K samplingRandomly samples from top-K optionsMore varied, natural-sounding text
Temperature 0.7–0.9Balanced randomnessMost chat applications including Claude
Temperature 1.5+Flat probabilities, creative outputCreative writing, brainstorming
Context window: The entire conversation history (system prompt + all previous turns + current generation) lives in the context window. Every generation step attends to everything before it. This is how models stay coherent across long responses — the self-attention mechanism allows any token to attend to any previous token in the window.

All Key Insights — One page summary
The most important takeaways from the entire conversation.
01 — Dot product: Measures direction agreement AND magnitude. Higher = more similar, but only relatively. Has no fixed range.
02 — Cosine similarity: Normalises out magnitude. Always between -1 and +1. Preferred for text search and RAG. Use dot product in Transformer attention for speed.
03 — Embeddings: Start as random numbers. Meaning emerges from backpropagation on context. 768D dimensions are entangled — no single dimension has a clean meaning.
04 — BERT training: Predicts masked words (classification task). Ground truth is always a word label, never a vector. The vector is a side effect — learned because it helps predict correctly.
05 — Self-attention: Every token queries every other token via Q·K dot product. Attention weights determine how much of each Value vector to incorporate. Wq, Wk, Wv are the learned parameters (768×768 each).
06 — Layer specialisation: Never configured. Early layers capture syntax, late layers capture semantics. Emergent from depth — each layer builds on the previous.
07 — Decoder-only: Still has an embedding table. Still converts text to vectors. "No separate encoder" means no dedicated encoder component — not that embedding is skipped.
08 — Modern LLMs: Moved from dense to MoE. Context windows grew from 512 (BERT) to 1M tokens. Architecture details are largely not disclosed publicly by frontier labs.
